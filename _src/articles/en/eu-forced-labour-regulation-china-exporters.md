**Short answer:** From 14 December 2027, no product made in whole or in part with forced labour may be placed on, sold in or exported from the EU market (Regulation (EU) 2024/3015). It applies to every product and every company, whatever its size, sector or country. There is no exemption for SMEs. If authorities find a breach, the product can be banned, withdrawn and disposed of across the whole EU.

## Key dates

| Date | Milestone |
| --- | --- |
| 13 Dec 2024 | Regulation entered into force |
| Jun 2026 | Commission guidelines due on due diligence and risk indicators |
| 14 Dec 2026 | Member states set their national penalties |
| 14 Dec 2027 | Ban applies to companies |

## What it does and does not require

- **It does not create a new due-diligence law.** But in an investigation, a company must show what it did to identify, prevent and end forced-labour risk in its supply chain.
- **It covers every stage.** Extraction, processing and manufacture, inside or outside the EU. A battery is caught if forced labour touched its graphite, lithium or cells, not just the final assembly.
- **It covers everyone who sells.** Manufacturers, importers, distributors and online sellers targeting EU buyers. Your European customer is exposed, so they will ask you for evidence.
- **Services are out of scope.** Transport, warehousing and logistics are not covered; the goods are.

## What a Chinese exporter should prepare

1. **A supplier map to raw-material level** for your top product lines, with addresses of each plant.
2. **Documents a buyer can hand to an authority:** supplier declarations, audit reports, origin certificates for high-risk inputs.
3. **A written policy and grievance channel** in Chinese and English, published on your website.
4. **One evidence pack per product**, reused across all EU buyers instead of answering each questionnaire from scratch.
5. **Contract clauses** that state who supplies what evidence, so a single investigation does not stop all your EU sales.

For battery makers this sits alongside the Batteries Regulation's supply-chain due diligence on cobalt, lithium, nickel and graphite, due 18 August 2027. Build one traceability system for both.
