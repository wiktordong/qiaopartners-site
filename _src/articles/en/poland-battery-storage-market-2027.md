**Short answer:** Poland is one of Europe's largest battery-storage markets, built on 17-year capacity contracts and a PLN 4.15 billion (about €1 billion) grant programme. Chinese suppliers already compete there: BYD Energy Storage will supply Poland's largest battery project, due by the end of 2027. But three changes since 2025 are shifting which projects a Chinese supplier can win, and how it must show up.

## What drives demand

- **Capacity market.** Battery projects won 165 MW of capacity obligations in the 2022 auction, 1.7 GW in 2023 and 2.5 GW in 2024. In the December 2025 auction the grid operator PSE cut the battery de-rating factor to about 13%, from 60% in 2024 and 95% in 2023. Batteries won fewer awards, and the capacity payment now covers a smaller share of a project's revenue.
- **Grants.** The Modernisation Fund programme run by NFOŚiGW drew requests of nearly PLN 28 billion, about seven times its budget. The final list supports 172 projects totalling 14.5 GWh, all due by the end of 2028.
- **State utilities.** PGE plans to invest about PLN 18 billion in storage over the next decade, adding 10 GWh for a 17 GWh portfolio.

## Three rule changes a Chinese supplier must plan for

| Rule | Status | Effect on Chinese suppliers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Net-Zero Industry Act, non-price criteria | Applies since 30 Dec 2025 | At least 30% of renewable-auction volume (or 6 GW a year) must score resilience, cybersecurity and responsible conduct, not only price |
| Industrial Accelerator Act, "Made in EU" | Proposed 4 Mar 2026; not yet law | Would require EU-origin BESS in public procurement and support schemes; the proposal adds an EU-made battery management system for systems above 1 MWh |
| Batteries Regulation passport | Mandatory 18 Feb 2027 | Every industrial battery above 2 kWh needs a digital passport |

## What this means in practice

1. **Merchant and capacity-market projects stay open.** Projects without public grants are bought mainly on price, bankability and warranty. Chinese suppliers remain competitive here.
2. **Grant-funded and public projects are closing.** If the Industrial Accelerator Act passes as proposed, subsidised projects will need EU-origin systems. Final assembly and the BMS in Poland is the route that keeps a Chinese supplier eligible.
3. **Assembly in Poland has a tax incentive.** The Polish Investment Zone grants a corporate income tax exemption for new manufacturing investments anywhere in Poland. From 1 January 2027 it is the only such scheme, as the old Special Economic Zones close at the end of 2026.
4. **Bankability decides the shortlist.** Lenders and developers typically ask for EU service presence, spare-parts stock and warranty backed by an EU entity. A Warsaw entity and service partner are often the price of entry.
